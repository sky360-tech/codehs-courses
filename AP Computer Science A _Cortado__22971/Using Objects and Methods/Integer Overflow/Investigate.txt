1. Which operation(s) resulted in integer overflow? 
Min minus 1 (becomes the maximum positive value).
Max plus 1
Max multiplied by 2

2. Why did these scenarios result in integer overflow?
In Java, an int uses 32 bits of memory. When you add 1 to the largest possible value, the binary math "carries" a 1 into the sign bit

3. Which operation(s) did not result in integer overflow? 
Min divided by 2.

4. Why did these scenarios not result in integer overflow?
Division by 2 makes the number smaller in magnitude (closer to zero). Since the original value was already within the allowed range, making it smaller ensures it stays within that range.

5. What could be a real-world scenario where an integer 
overflow error might occur?

Video Game Scores: If a player's score is stored as an int and they play long enough to exceed 2.14 billion points, their score could suddenly flip to a negative number. (This famously happened in early versions of Pac-Man and Donkey Kong).

YouTube View Counts: YouTube originally used a 32-bit integer to store view counts. When the "Gangnam Style" music video exceeded 2,147,483,647 views, it broke the counter, forcing YouTube to upgrade to a 64-bit long.

Bank Transactions: If a banking system uses integers to track cents and a massive transfer occurs, it could result in an overflow, causing money to "vanish" or balances to become negative.
